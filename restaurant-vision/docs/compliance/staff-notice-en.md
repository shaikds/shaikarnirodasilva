# Staff notice and monitoring policy

For: the restaurant as employer. Hebrew version: `staff-notice-he.md`.

| Part | Use it for |
|---|---|
| 1. Written policy (Israel, usable anywhere) | The specific written policy that Israeli law expects before cameras are used for a new purpose at work, with an acknowledgement form |
| 2. 14-day written notice (NSW and ACT, Australia) | The notice NSW and the ACT require at least 14 days before workplace surveillance starts |
| 3. Short notes for the US | Connecticut, New York, Delaware and California |

**The design rule this policy relies on.** Restaurant Vision measures tables and zones, never
people. No metric in the product is attributed to an employee, the "On it" button records no
identity, and there is no per-waiter report, ranking or score. If the restaurant ever wants
staff-level measurement, this policy no longer covers it: stop and get legal advice first
(Israel: Privacy Protection Authority guidance 5/2017 and *Isakov v. Panaya*; Australia: the
state workplace surveillance acts; California: CCPA risk assessments for "systematic
observation" of employees).

## Part 1. Written policy: AI service analytics on the restaurant's cameras (Israel)

Israeli background, for the person filling this in:

- Privacy Protection Authority guidance 5/2017 (cameras in the workplace) requires legitimacy,
  transparency, proportionality and good faith. Footage from cameras installed for security may
  not be repurposed to check attendance or output. No cameras in restrooms or changing rooms;
  employee consent does not cure this.
- *Isakov v. Panaya* (National Labour Court, 2011) requires a clear and specific written
  policy, consideration of less invasive alternatives and, for monitoring, informed, free and
  written consent.
- Amendment 13 to the Privacy Protection Law (in force 14 Aug 2025) defines personal data
  broadly and strengthens enforcement.

This policy therefore states one specific purpose, measures nothing per employee, and records
each employee's signed acknowledgement.

### [RESTAURANT LEGAL NAME]: policy on AI service analytics

**Effective from:** [DATE] · **Branch(es):** [BRANCHES] · **Policy owner:** [NAME, ROLE]

**1. What is being introduced.** From [START DATE] the restaurant uses Restaurant Vision, an AI
system from [VENDOR LEGAL NAME], which analyses the live video of the following existing
cameras: [LIST: camera name and area, e.g. "Dining room A (ceiling)", "Entrance"]. The software
runs on a computer inside the restaurant ([LOCATION OF THE PC]).

**2. The only purpose.** Running service for guests: showing which tables are waiting for a
first visit, a check-in or clearing; sending table alerts to the waiters' phones; measuring
the entrance queue, waiting times and walk-aways; counting guests and table turnover; and
producing shift reports about the restaurant as a whole. The system is not used for security,
attendance, timekeeping, discipline or evaluating any employee.

**3. What the system measures.** Only states and times of tables and zones, for example "table 7
seated 4 minutes ago and not yet visited", "table 3 needs clearing", "queue: 5 people, longest
wait 8 minutes", "average first-visit time this evening: 3 minutes". To know whether a table was
visited, the software distinguishes staff from guests (by the uniform's colour, by movement
pattern and by the staff area), without identifying anyone.

**4. What the system does not measure or do.**

- It does not identify anyone, does not recognise faces and keeps no image of any person.
- It does not attribute any action, time or count to an individual employee. The "On it"
  button does not record who pressed it. There are no per-waiter reports, scores or rankings.
- It does not record video or sound, and keeps no images.
- It does not track an employee between cameras or between days. Its short-term movement labels
  are random and are discarded at least every 24 hours.
- It does not detect emotions, fatigue, breaks or attendance.
- It does not cover restrooms, changing rooms or staff rooms. These areas are masked in the
  software (blacked out before analysis) and no camera of this system points at them.

**5. How the results are used.** Results are used at table, zone and shift level only, for
example to decide staffing levels for a busy evening, to change the floor layout, or to improve
how alerts reach the team. They will not be used, alone or with other information, to evaluate,
reward, warn, discipline or dismiss any employee, and will not be offered as evidence about an
individual employee.

**6. Why this method (proportionality).** The restaurant considered manual observation by a
manager (less consistent, more intrusive in practice), data from the POS only (does not show
waiting tables or the queue) and systems with face recognition or recorded video (rejected as
too intrusive). It chose the least intrusive option that meets the purpose: anonymous analysis
inside the restaurant, with numbers only leaving it.

**7. Who sees what.** Managers ([ROLES]) see live table states, alerts and restaurant-level
reports in the dashboard. Waiters see open table alerts on their phones. [VENDOR LEGAL NAME]
processes the anonymous data on the restaurant's behalf and has no access to video.

**8. Retention.** No video or images are kept by this system. Anonymous table and zone events
are kept for 90 days, and reports derived from them for 400 days (about 13 months) [adjust if
your agreement sets other periods]; then they are deleted automatically.

**9. The existing security cameras** remain governed by the restaurant's camera policy
[REFERENCE]. This policy does not widen what the cameras record or who may watch recordings.

**10. Questions, objections and the workers' committee.** Questions or concerns go to [NAME,
CONTACT]. [If there is a workers' committee or union: the committee was consulted on [DATE]
and its comments were [SUMMARY].] Raising a concern will not harm any employee.

**11. Changes.** A new purpose, any measurement of individual employees, new cameras or areas,
or any recording requires a new written policy and fresh notice to employees before it starts
(and new written consent where the law requires it).

### Employee acknowledgement and consent

I, the undersigned, received the policy "AI service analytics on the restaurant's cameras"

## Part 2. 14-day written notice of workplace surveillance (NSW and ACT, Australia)

Background: the *Workplace Surveillance Act 2005* (NSW), s.10, requires written notice to each
employee at least 14 days before camera surveillance starts (an employee may agree to a shorter
period), cameras that are clearly visible and signs visible at each entrance to the workplace.
The *Workplace Privacy Act 2011* (ACT) also requires 14 days' written notice and good-faith
consultation with workers before surveillance starts. Give this notice to every employee in
writing (email or letter) at least 14 days before the start date, and to new employees before
they start. Keep proof of delivery.

> **[EMPLOYER LEGAL NAME], ABN [ABN]**
> **Notice of workplace surveillance: AI service analytics**
>
> Date of notice: [DATE]
> To: [EMPLOYEE NAME] / all employees at [VENUE ADDRESS]
>
> This notice is given under section 10 of the *Workplace Surveillance Act 2005* (NSW) /
> the *Workplace Privacy Act 2011* (ACT) [delete one].
>
> 1. **Kind of surveillance.** Camera surveillance. The venue's existing cameras listed below
>    will be analysed by AI software (Restaurant Vision, supplied by [VENDOR LEGAL NAME]) that
>    detects the presence of people, without identifying them, to produce anonymous table and
>    queue information.
>    Cameras: [LIST: location and area covered].
> 2. **How it will be carried out.** The software runs on a computer inside the venue. Video is
>    processed in memory and is not recorded or stored by this system and does not leave the
>    venue. The software produces only anonymous events and numbers about tables, the entrance
>    queue and guest counts (for example "table 7 needs clearing"). It distinguishes staff from
>    guests to know whether a table has been visited, but does not identify anyone. No
>    information is attributed to any individual employee, there is no audio, and toilets,
>    change rooms and staff rooms are excluded.
> 3. **Start date.** [START DATE: at least 14 days after the date of this notice]
> 4. **Continuous or intermittent.** Continuous during opening hours [or: intermittent, during
>    service periods: HOURS].
> 5. **Period.** Ongoing until further notice [or: for a trial period from [DATE] to [DATE]].
> 6. **Purpose and use.** Guest service operations: table alerts, waiting times and shift
>    reports at venue level. The information will not be used to evaluate, discipline or make
>    decisions about any individual employee.
> 7. **Signs and cameras.** The cameras are clearly visible and signs are displayed at each
>    entrance to the workplace.
> 8. **Consultation (ACT) and questions.** We invite your comments or concerns before the start
>    date: contact [NAME, CONTACT] by [DATE]. [ACT: a consultation meeting will be held on
>    [DATE].]
>
> Signed: [NAME, POSITION]

Acknowledgement (optional, recommended):

| Employee name | Date received | Signature |
|---|---|---|
| | | |

Other states: Victoria has announced but not enacted workplace surveillance reform (as of late
2026); give the same notice as best practice. Check SA, WA, QLD surveillance laws with counsel.

## Part 3. Short notes for the United States

- **Connecticut** (Gen. Stat. § 31-48d): prior written notice of electronic monitoring "by any
  means other than direct observation", which covers camera analytics, plus a notice posted
  in a conspicuous place. Part 1 sections 1 to 8 can serve as the notice.
- **New York** (Civ. Rights Law § 52-c): aimed at phone, email and internet monitoring; written
  notice at hiring with an acknowledgement is best practice here too (use the Part 1 form).
- **Delaware** (19 Del. C. § 705): similar notice rules for electronic monitoring.
- **California**: from 1 Jan 2027 (AB 1331) no workplace surveillance in restrooms, changing or
  lactation areas: keep those areas masked. If the restaurant is subject to the CCPA, inferring
  employee performance from video needs a risk assessment; this system avoids that by design
  (table level only), so keep it that way.
- Post the customer sign (`signage-en.md`) as well: staff pass it too, and it repeats in one
  line what the system does and does not do.
