## Q1. Market size and growth: restaurant tech, restaurant analytics, video analytics, AI in food service (global/US/AU)

### Takeaway
No analyst sizes our exact category: anonymous, camera-based FOH analytics for full-service restaurants. The adjacent markets are large and growing fast. Global video analytics is about US$11–12B in 2025 at a ~20%+ CAGR. "Restaurant analytics" is US$1–6B, depending on the publisher's definition. AI in QSR is about US$1–5B. ### Inferences
- **Methodology limits:** These reports are top-down (vendor revenue plus analyst assumptions), paywalled, with inconsistent scopes (software only vs. hardware+POS, QSR only vs. all foodservice, robotics included or not). Base years differ, and they are often recycled through aggregator sites (GII, MarketResearch.com, MarketPublishers). The order-of-magnitude spread for the same label (US$1B to US$6B for "restaurant analytics", US$11B to US$110B for "video analytics") means they **should not be used as a TAM for this product**. Cite them only as "adjacent markets growing ~10–25%/yr."
- There is no Australia-specific figure for restaurant tech or video analytics market size. AU should be sized bottom-up (see Q2).
- **[EST]** If US restaurant tech spend is ~3% of US$1.2T (eating and drinking places), the total is ~US$36B/yr. That is the wallet all restaurant tech competes for. FOH analytics would be a small line item within it.

### Gaps

## Q2. Number of full-service restaurants and chains (US & AU); revenue per location; tech spend; SaaS spend per location

### Takeaway
The US has roughly 150k single-location full-service restaurants (IBISWorld) and about 240k full-service *independents* (derived from Technomic). On top of that are an unknown but large number of full-service chain units. Full-service independents are shrinking (−2.6% in 2025) while full-service chains are flat. Australia has about 29–30k restaurant businesses plus about 28k cafés. Revenue per location ranges from ~US$1.7M (US independents) to US$4.5–12.8M (top casual chains), and ~A$0.9M per restaurant business in AU.

### Inferences
- **[EST] US full-service independents ≈ 240k at end-2025.** Derivation: a net loss of 6,400 equal to 2.6% implies a base of ~246k at end-2024, minus 6,400. This number conflicts with IBISWorld's 152k "single-location FSR", probably because of definitions (Technomic's "independent" can include small multi-unit groups and bars with food). Treat the **US FSR independent universe as ~150–240k**.
- **[EST] US full-service chain units can't be derived reliably** from the "−0.1% = −165 units" rounding, since that implies anywhere from ~110k to 330k. Industry-wide, full-service chain units are commonly in the tens of thousands; this needs Technomic or Circana data (see Gaps).
- **[EST] Revenue per US independent FSR ≈ US$1.7M** (US$253.9B / 150.1k, Oysterlink). Top casual chains run 3–7x that.
- **[EST] Revenue per AU restaurant business ≈ A$0.89M** (A$26.1B / 29.4k, IBISWorld).
- **[EST] Affordability math for a US$1.7M independent FSR:** labor at 36.5% is ~US$620k/yr. Pre-tax profit at 2.8% is ~US$48k/yr. All tech at <3% of sales is <US$51k/yr. A **US$200/location/month** product (US$2.4k/yr) equals **~5% of annual pre-tax profit**, so it needs clear ROI: e.g., saving ~0.4% of labor cost (~US$2.4k), or recovering ~1–2 lost covers per day of walk-aways.
- **[EST] Illustrative bottom-up SAM** (not a forecast; key inputs are uncertain):
  - **US:** ~40–60k FSR locations with adequate IP camera coverage and a ≥US$2M revenue profile (mid-to-upper independents plus multi-unit casual and fine dining) × US$150–300/mo ≈ **US$70–220M ARR**.
  - **AU:** ~5–8k suitable restaurant venues (out of ~29k restaurants plus some table-service cafés) × A$150–300/mo ≈ **A$9–29M ARR**.
  - The share of FSRs with usable IP cameras is unknown (see Gaps).

## Q3. Adoption of AI / computer vision among restaurants (surveys)

### Takeaway
AI use among US operators is still early: about 26% use any AI tool. Full-service use is mostly marketing (19%). Operators intend to invest (60% plan more customer-experience tech spend), but proven ROI is rare (only 5% of QSR/fast-casual brands report measurable value from AI). Australian figures are higher but rest on inconsistent definitions. Camera-based FOH analytics adoption is not measured in any survey found.

### Cited Findings
- NRA State of the Restaurant Industry 2026 (released Feb 2026): **26% of operators use AI-related tools**. **10%** use AI for some administrative tasks. Only **6%** use AI for customer orders. **Marketing is the top use case: 19% of full-service and 15% of limited-service operators.** About 1 in 4 limited-service operators plan to invest in kitchen automation or AI inventory tracking (snippet) — [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/national-restaurant-assocation-artificial-intelligence-adoption/812418/) (snippet)
- NRA 2026: **60%** of operators say their tech use is in line with competitors, **12%** say leading edge and **28%** say lagging. **6 in 10 plan to invest more in technology to enhance customer experience.** **22%** say they struggle to find talent (snippet) — [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/national-restaurant-assocation-artificial-intelligence-adoption/812418/) (snippet)
- Qu 2026 Restaurant Technology Benchmark (168 fast-casual and QSR brands): **73% are investing in AI or plan to start in 2026, but only 5% report measurable operational value or guest impact from AI to date** (snippet; press release fetch blocked) — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260319388430/en/Restaurants-Boost-AI-and-Tech-Investment-Amid-Margin-Pressure-But-Operational-Gaps-Persist) (snippet)
- Hospitality Technology's 2026 Restaurant Technology Study is gated. Its teaser copy says finance teams are pushing toward "solutions with provable ROI" — [HT 2026 RTS landing page](https://fn-resources.fierce-network.com/free-offer/the-tech-leaders-guide-to-the-2026-restaurant-technology-study/w_hosq03?sr=hicat)

No AU-only split was found — [Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/the-bottom-line/operating-your-business/ai-in-restaurants-report)
- AU, SevenRooms report: **85% of Australian hospitality operators "leveraging AI"** (vendor; definition broad) — [eCommerceNews AU](https://ecommercenews.com.au/story/australian-hospitality-sector-embraces-ai-to-enhance-efficiency) (vendor)
- AU, Australian AI Adoption Tracker (cited by R&CA): **42% of SME hospitality businesses use AI** — [R&CA](https://www.rca.asn.au/homepage-news-gallery/ai-in-hospitality-whats-changing-in-2025)
- AU, Planday 2025 Tech Trends: only **17% of operators fully understand AI's potential impact** — [eCommerceNews AU](https://ecommercenews.com.au/story/planday-how-ai-tech-will-change-hospitality-by-2025)
- 2026 NRA Show coverage: AI is described as moving from experiments to daily use, with nearly one-third of operators seeing AI as one of the biggest tech opportunities in 2026 (trade and vendor-linked) — [Restaurant Technology News](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/what-the-2026-national-restaurant-association-show-revealed-about-the-future-of-restaurant-technology/)
- Toast IQ (Toast's AI layer, June 2026): **margin protection** is the top use case among operators (vendor) — [BusinessWire/Toast](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20260610036506/en/90-Days-with-Toast-IQ-How-Restaurant-Operators-Are-Using-Toast-IQ-to-Find-Time-Protect-Margins-and-Grow) (snippet)
- **Computer-vision FOH precedents** (to show adoption is pilot-stage):
  - Outback Steakhouse piloted **Presto Vision** with lobby and dining-room cameras that alert managers and servers via wearables. Press framed it as "Big Brother meets Bloomin' Onion" — [The Takeout](https://www.thetakeout.com:443/outback-ai-nannycam-surveillance-presto-vision-1839103568)
  - SciForce built a custom CV system for a ~1,200-location chain. It maps tables to camera zones and **alerts when no waiter approaches a new table within 3 minutes** (vendor case study) — [SciForce](https://www.sciforce.solutions/case-studies/computer-vision-restaurant-service-optimization) (vendor)
  - Interface Systems (managed services) launched AI video analytics with Wobot for multi-site retail and restaurant chains (June 2024) — [Retail Dive press release](https://www.retaildive.com/press-release/20240626-interface-systems-unveils-ai-powered-video-analytics-solution-in-partnershi-1)

### Inferences
- The adoption gap is a **proof gap, not an interest gap**: high stated intent (60–85%) but very low measured value (5%). The MVP must make ROI visible in the dashboard (covers recovered, minutes saved, labor hours).
- Full-service operators currently use AI mainly for marketing. Operational CV is a new category, so expect education-heavy selling and pilots.
- Australian AI adoption figures (42% vs. 85%) are not comparable. Don't use them as headline numbers without caveats.

## Q4. Top operator pain points 2025–2026 and which have quantified revenue/cost impact

### Takeaway
Labor is the dominant pain. FSR labor is ~36.5% of sales against ~2.8% pre-tax margins, and 22% of US operators struggle to find talent. Guest-wait pain is quantified at the diner level: ~30% have left before ordering because of long waits, and 25% would consider leaving if the bill takes >30 min. Cornell (Kimes) research supports revenue gains from shorter, more predictable dining duration, but **only when demand exceeds capacity** (peak periods). Server tips are only weakly tied to service quality. "Staff response time" is best sold as a guest-experience and labor-productivity lever, not a tip lever.

### Cited Findings
**Labor (cost and shortage)**
- US FSR median labor is **36.5% of sales** (FY2024). The gap between profitable (34.2%) and unprofitable (42.9%) FSRs is **8.7 points of sales** (secondary citation of NRA) — [NRA analysis](https://restaurant.org/research-and-media/research/restaurant-economic-insights/analysis-commentary/restaurant-labor-costs-are-well-above-historical-averages/); [Templi](https://www.templi.com/blog/labor-cost-percentages-for-restaurants)
- NRA 2026: **22%** of operators struggle to find talent; the industry expects to add 100k+ jobs in 2026 — [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/national-restaurant-assocation-artificial-intelligence-adoption/812418/) (snippet); [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/operations/restaurant-foodservice-sales-are-expected-reach-155t-2026)
- AU: wages are **~28% of turnover** and **~30% of businesses were loss-making in 2023–24** (R&CA 2024) — [R&CA](https://www.rca.asn.au/homepage-news-gallery/why-the-benchmarking-report-matters)
- AU: the hospitality vacancy rate fell from a **~17% peak (mid-2023) to ~11% (late 2025)** (blog citing R&CA; secondary) — [Pondering Pot](https://ponderingpot.com.au/blog/2025-12-27-restaurant-labour-shortage-whats-changed) (secondary)
- AU 2026: industry bodies warn of a wage rise, energy costs and tighter margins (headline only) — [QSR Media](https://qsrmedia.com/indonesian/node/514602669) (snippet)

**Guest wait, walk-aways and service speed**
- Lightspeed survey of 1,000 US diners: **~30% have left a restaurant before ordering because of long waits**. **27%** cite long waits as a reason not to return, against 40% for rude service and 35% for cold food — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/408597/report-rude-service-cold-food-long-wait-times-d.html)
- Toast survey: **72% of diners will wait only up to 30 minutes for a table** — [Chowhound](https://www.chowhound.com/stories/70-of-diners-wait-this-long-for-a-restaurant-table)
- Long Range Systems (older study, date unclear): average party wait is **23 min**, and **nearly one-third of parties wait >30 min** — [Hospitality Technology](https://hospitalitytech.com/study-reveals-average-restaurant-wait-times)
- Survey of 2,000 people: **25% would consider leaving if they waited >30 min for the bill** (check-presentation delay) — [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/food-drink/1-in-20-diners-has-left-a-restaurant-without-paying-study-finds.amp) (attribution per snippet)
- 2017 survey (QikServe-related): **93%** of consumers have left or walked past a restaurant because of long lines (old, QSR-skewed) — [Modern Restaurant Management](https://modernrestaurantmanagement.com/according-to-a-recent-study-survey-midyear-2017-edition/)

**Table turn / dining duration (Cornell, Kimes)**
- Kimes (1999, as cited in later work): in a **hypothetical 100-seat restaurant with a $20 check, 1-hour average dining time and a busy peak, shortening meals would raise revenue ~20%** (illustrative, not empirical) — [Cornell eCommons](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e4a944d8-bdad-4220-9281-df8203ba1e8c/content) (snippet)
- Kimes & Wirtz, "How long should dinner take?": a pilot study suggests many restaurants could **shorten dining duration by ~20% without hurting customer satisfaction** — [Cornell eCommons](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/2baf3e15-cfd5-403d-9397-793aec70f8cc/content) (snippet)
- Kimes & Robson (2004) analysed **1,400+ transactions at a 210-seat casual Mexican restaurant**. Booths had slightly above-average spend per minute (SPM), banquettes below average, and "poor-location" tables had *higher* SPM than "good" tables. Table mix and seat assignment matter — [HospitalityNet / Cornell release](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4018612.html)
- Chevys Arrowhead case (Kimes 2004): reworking layout and staffing raised revenue, and the occupancy-driven revenue gain **paid back the capital cost within one year**, with menu prices unchanged — [AsiaTravelTips / Cornell release](https://www.asiatraveltips.com/travelnews04/182Cornell.shtml)
- Kimes et al. (2009, Cornell Hospitality Quarterly): common RevPASH approximations are inaccurate, **"much greater when RevPASH is calculated based on check-open times"** rather than the true seating interval — [Cornell eCommons](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/519ba198-3b5b-4fa3-84ca-042677dd7a5e)
- A counterpoint paper exists: "The mythical revenue benefits of reducing dining duration in restaurants" (not read; the title suggests the benefits are conditional) — [Typeset](https://typeset.io/pdf/mythical-revenue-benefits-of-reducing-dining-duration-in-2wke3n4f8i.pdf) (title only)
- Upserve data (via NRN): **~45 minutes is the "optimal" table time**; tips rise with time only up to ~40–50 min — [1851 Franchise / NRN](https://1851franchise.com/nations-restaurant-news-data-the-optimal-table-turnover-time-is-45-minutes-2705367)
- Vendor-blog benchmarks: dinner turns of **60–90 min**, fine dining **90–120 min** (unverified) — [Tablein](https://tablein.com/blog/table-turnover-guide) (vendor)

**Tips and staff accountability**
- Lynn (2001) meta-analysis of **24 correlations from 14 studies**: the link between service quality and tip size is **"tenuous at best"**, which undermines tips as a measure of server performance — [Cornell eCommons](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/042e8740-5c44-486e-ab28-00f05781d153)
- Lynn (2003) update: service accounts for **less than 2% of the variation** in a restaurant's tip percentages (partial abstract; snippet) — [Cornell eCommons](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/38a5f856-fd72-4880-af19-c0296552f1bb/content) (snippet)
- Lynn & Sturman (2010), 51 diners over repeated visits: within-person tip sizes **are** reliably correlated with service ratings — [Cornell eCommons](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/b7f993ca-5cf1-4763-9b86-2b61afc714d9) (snippet; attribution approximate)

### Inferences
- **Feature-to-value mapping (prioritisation):**
  1. **Entrance queue, wait time and walk-away detection:** directly quantifiable lost revenue (walk-aways × average check) at peak, which is where Kimes says duration and capacity management pays. This is a strong revenue story.
  2. **Per-table state, especially "waiting for check" and "needs bussing":** cuts the dead time at the end of a meal and lowers the reseat interval. This is the most controllable portion of dining duration, and it addresses a real walk-out risk (25% would consider leaving after a >30 min bill wait).
  3. **Labor/staff productivity (response times, covers per server, section balance):** labor is the #1 P&L line (36.5%). The value is better deployment at the same headcount; operators will not accept "fewer staff" claims without proof.
  4. **True seat-occupancy and turn data:** Kimes shows that POS check-open/close times misstate RevPASH. Camera-measured seating intervals are a **differentiated data asset** that POS cannot provide.
  5. **Tip outcomes:** weak link per Lynn, so don't lead with "more tips." Position response time as guest satisfaction and repeat visits instead.
- Table-turn ROI applies **only to capacity-constrained periods and venues** (busy dinner, weekends). Off-peak turn acceleration has little revenue value. Target busy venues with waitlists.
- **[EST] Illustrative ROI:** a 150-seat casual FSR with a US$30 check and a ~20% faster peak turn on two busy services/week could add on the order of tens of thousands of US$/yr, if demand exists. That dwarfs a US$2–4k/yr subscription. Validate with pilot data.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous study quantifying **"server response time → revenue/check size"** in full-service restaurants.
- I found no 2025–2026 empirical data on walk-away rates measured in-venue, as opposed to self-reported surveys.
- I found no peer-reviewed number of the form "reducing table turn by X minutes = Y% revenue" beyond Kimes's hypotheticals and case studies. Original papers could not be fully read.
- Server-to-table ratio benchmarks were not found from a reliable source.

## Q5. Buyer personas, who signs, sales cycle, pilot expectations

### Takeaway
Persona evidence is thin and mostly from vendor or consultant blogs and job postings. The consistent pattern:
- **Independents and small groups:** buy through the owner-operator, with fast cycles (weeks).
- **Emerging multi-unit brands (10–100 units):** buy through the VP/Director of Operations or Director of Technology, with cycles of ~1–3 months.
- **Enterprise chains:** buy through the COO, CFO and IT/security by committee, with **6–18 month** cycles gated by paid pilots, security review and POS integration.

### Cited Findings
- Consultant blog: sales cycles run **3–9 months at multi-unit operators** and **9–18 months at enterprise chains/QSRs**, with independents "much faster" (consultant; not primary research) — [SaaS Hero](https://www.saashero.net/strategy/build-restaurant-tech-sales-funnel/)
- Consultant blog: emerging multi-unit brands (10–100 units) run **4–10 week** cycles, with a **VP of Operations or Director of Technology** typically deciding. Enterprise chain deals run **6–15 months** from qualified opportunity to signature, driven by **security review, integration scoping, pilots and committee approval** (consultant; snippet) — [Pulse RevOps](https://pulserevops.com/revenue-architecture/ra0015) (snippet)
- Mid-market groups buy through the owner-operator directly, at contract values in the tens of thousands; a target list for 10–200 unit operators is **President + Director of Operations + VP Marketing** (consultant; snippet) — [SaaS Hero GTM](https://saashero.net/strategy/restaurant-tech-gtm-strategies/) (snippet)
- Pilot-to-rollout conversion reportedly **53% with documented unit-economics impact vs. 20% without** (attributed to a Toast study; unverified) — [SaaS Hero](https://www.saashero.net/strategy/build-restaurant-tech-sales-funnel/) (unverified)
- Job postings for enterprise restaurant-tech AEs name the **COO** as a buyer at chains with **US$500M–US$10B+ revenue**, and treat **new CFO/COO/VP hires at multi-unit groups as trigger events** — [Otter job posting (Built In)](https://builtin.com/job/strategic-enterprise-account-executive-otter-los-angeles/7845956); [Palona Enterprise AE – Multi-Unit Restaurants](https://builtin.com/job/enterprise-account-executive-multi-unit-restaurants/7429656)
- Agot AI, a CV vendor, typically serves customers with **≥2,000 restaurant locations**. It raised a US$10M seed and lists "Table Turn Acceleration" among its full-service features — [Ensun profile](https://ensun.io/company/agotai-649d356feb49834ed9132404)
- Solink, the closest comparable for restaurant video plus POS analytics, offers **month-to-month with no cancellation fee or multi-year agreements**, and **pilots are available** — [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/ca/physical-security/solink-profile/)
- Hospitality Technology's 2026 RTS is aimed at "restaurant executives, IT leaders and operators" and has separate CFO, operations and tech-leader guides, which implies a multi-stakeholder purchase in larger chains — [HT 2026 RTS landing page](https://fn-resources.fierce-network.com/free-offer/the-tech-leaders-guide-to-the-2026-restaurant-technology-study/w_hosq03?sr=hicat)
- A consultant blog claims **73% of operators increased tech spending in 2025, but only 28% say tech improved profitability** (unverified) — [SaaS Hero](https://www.saashero.net/strategy/restaurant-tech-saas-marketing/) (unverified)

### Inferences
- **Recommended persona priority for MVP:**
  1. **Multi-unit independent groups (3–30 FSR venues)**, owner/CEO plus Director of Ops. Short cycle, the decision maker feels the labor and wait pain personally, and they have existing cameras.
  2. **Emerging casual-dining and premium-casual chains (10–100 units)**, VP Ops plus Director of Tech, with a 1–3 location paid pilot of 30–90 days.
  3. **Hotel F&B and food halls**: likely longer IT/security reviews. I found no specific data.
  4. **Enterprise chains**: 9–18 months with security review; pursue after reference customers.
- Pilots should be designed to output **unit-economics proof** (minutes saved, walk-aways avoided, check-close delay), because conversion reportedly depends on it.
- The economic buyer is Ops (COO/VP Ops/owner). IT is a gatekeeper because of camera network access and edge hardware. Privacy and legal (especially in AU) may be a veto point.

### Gaps
- No neutral or primary data on sales-cycle length or pilot duration for restaurant analytics.
- No data on buyer personas specifically for hotel F&B, food halls or franchise groups.
- No AU-specific procurement data.

## Q7. Objections: guest/staff privacy, surveillance perceptions, accuracy, installation burden

### Takeaway
Privacy and surveillance perception is the biggest non-technical risk, and it is **sharper in Australia**:
- The OAIC ruled that both Bunnings and Kmart broke the Privacy Act with facial recognition, and the tribunal upheld aspects of the Bunnings ruling in March 2026.
- NSW requires 14 days' written notice to staff before workplace camera surveillance.

The product's "fully anonymous, no face recognition, no identity" design is a material differentiator, but the staff-monitoring features (response times, accountability) will trigger worker pushback. Surveys show 80% of workers are uncomfortable with camera monitoring and 73% say it damages trust.

### Cited Findings
**Australia regulatory**
- OAIC (Privacy Commissioner Carly Kind) found **Bunnings breached the Privacy Act** by collecting facial biometric (sensitive) data without consent. Bunnings was ordered to destroy retained data, publish a statement and not use the technology again — [CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/consumers-and-data/data-collection-and-use/how-your-data-is-used/articles/oaic-finding-against-bunnings); [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/bunnings-breached-australian-privacy-laws-with-facial-recognition-tech-613207)
- The Administrative Review Tribunal (**March 2026**) affirmed aspects of the Nov 2024 Bunnings determination and confirmed a "high bar" for FRT. The OAIC then updated its retail FRT guidance. That guidance stresses **necessity and proportionality, notice and consent, governance, and considering less-intrusive alternatives**. It is explicitly **not a ban** — [OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/news/media-centre/privacy-commissioner-publishes-updated-guidance-on-facial-recognition-in-retail-spaces)
- Kmart was found in breach for FRT in **28 stores (June 2020–July 2022)**. The determination is dated Aug or Sept 2025 (sources differ) and is under ART review — [Clayton Utz](https://www.claytonutz.com/insights/2025/november/from-cameras-to-compliance-oaics-kmart-determination-highlights-facial-recognition-technology-privacy-risks); [Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/australia/oaic-determines-kmart-breached-the-privacy-act-1988-(cth)-over-use-of-facial-recognition-technology); [SBS](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/unlawful-kmarts-facial-recognition-tech-breached-australians-privacy-watchdog-finds/p8vjn5rjw)
- NSW Workplace Surveillance Act 2005:
  - **Written notice at least 14 days before camera surveillance starts** (an employee may agree to less), or before a new hire starts
  - Notice must state the type, method, start date and whether surveillance is continuous
  - **Cameras must be visible, with signage at entrances**
  - No surveillance in toilets or change rooms
  - Recording private conversations also falls under the Surveillance Devices Act 2007

  [NSW Legislation](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/2024-07-01/act-2005-047); [Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/nsw-workplace-surveillance-act-compliance-checklist-for-employers/)
- UTS (March 2026, Australian polling): **75% of workers say monitoring should not be used unless clearly communicated and strictly necessary**. Top concerns are audio surveillance (72%), live-screen monitoring (71%) and **emotion recognition (70%)** — [UTS](https://www.uts.edu.au/news/2026/03/australian-law-must-protect-workers-from-harmful-tech-enabled-surveillance)

**Worker perceptions (UK/US, mostly 2021–2023)**
- Prospect (UK): **80% of workers would be uncomfortable with camera monitoring** and 66% with keystroke monitoring — [Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/252490103/Employees-overwhelmingly-hostile-to-workplace-monitoring-tech)
- TUC (UK): **60%** believe they have been monitored at work, and **82% support a legal requirement to consult before introducing monitoring** — [ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/business/policy-legislation/363929/majority-of-workers-experienced-surveillance-in-the-past-year)
- CIPD (UK): **73%** say workplace monitoring tech would damage trust between workers and employers — [UK Parliament written evidence (CIPD)](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/125628/html/)
- APA (US, 2023): **26% of monitored workers feel not valued at work, vs. 17% of unmonitored workers** — [APA](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2023/09/artificial-intelligence-poor-mental-health)

**Guest perceptions**
- Toast (850 guests, July 2026): **48–49% are comfortable** with restaurants using visible AI and 23% are uncomfortable. Data privacy is only **12%** of main worries. **What matters most is whether a human stays involved** — [Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/es-us/blog/data/do-restaurant-guests-trust-ai)
- HungerRush (Nov 2025): **56% worry about data privacy**, behind losing human interaction (63%) and tech failures (58%) — [Galveston Daily News / BusinessWire](https://www.galvnews.com/new-hungerrush-data-reveals-diners-want-ai-to-enhance-not-replace-the-human-touch/article_55ce93a0-b66e-5ca2-aaee-782284a9a07c.html)
- Intouch Insight (drive-thru): **62% are not fans of AI voice and video tech**; privacy intrusion is the top reason — [Modern Restaurant Management](https://modernrestaurantmanagement.com/how-customers-really-feel-about-ai-at-drive-thrus/)
- Press reaction to Outback's camera pilot: "Big Brother Meets Bloomin' Onion" — [The Takeout](https://www.thetakeout.com:443/outback-ai-nannycam-surveillance-presto-vision-1839103568)

**Accuracy / ROI / installation**
- Only **5%** of QSR and fast-casual brands report measurable value from AI so far (Qu 2026; snippet) — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260319388430/en/Restaurants-Boost-AI-and-Tech-Investment-Amid-Margin-Pressure-But-Operational-Gaps-Persist) (snippet)
- Hardware-led AI video installs run **US$1,500–5,000/location** (Spot AI) — [Get Safe and Sound](https://getsafeandsound.com/blog/spot-ai-pricing/)
- Vendor accuracy claims (e.g., Shire's "99.7%") are unverified — [Shire demo site](https://shire-frontend.vercel.app)

### Inferences
- **Positioning:** "No faces, no identities, no biometrics; on-device processing; only aggregate table and queue states leave the venue." In AU, this keeps the product outside the "sensitive information/biometric" category that sank Bunnings and Kmart. It still needs a Privacy Impact Assessment template, signage kit and NSW 14-day staff-notice template as part of onboarding.
- **Staff framing:** present response-time metrics as **section/team-level service support** ("table 12 needs someone"), not individual surveillance or discipline scores. Offer a configurable "no per-employee attribution" mode. Avoid emotion and audio analysis entirely, since emotion recognition and audio are workers' top concerns (70–72%).
- **Guest framing:** guests care more about keeping humans in the loop than about AI itself. "Helps your server notice you sooner" is a guest-positive message.
- **Accuracy:** buyers are burned by unproven AI (5% measurable value). Publish per-state precision/recall from pilots and show a "confidence" indicator. False waiter alerts will quickly erode staff trust.
- **Installation:** BYOC plus a single edge box is a real advantage over US$1.5–5k hardware installs. However, camera coverage and angles of existing CCTV, which is usually set up for security at entrances and tills rather than dining tables, are a likely technical objection.

### Gaps
- US state-level laws were not researched: Illinois BIPA, CCPA/CPRA, the NY biometric signage law, and workplace notice laws in CT/DE/NY.
- No hospitality union (e.g., UNITE HERE, United Workers Union AU) positions on in-venue camera analytics were found.
- No data was found on how many FSRs have existing IP cameras with dining-floor coverage. This is critical for the BYOC model.
- No independent accuracy benchmarks exist for table-state detection in real restaurants.
